Dialogue Engine rules:
- Every key beat must include speakerName, characterWant, tactic, subtext, dialogue, and voiceExecution.
- Dialogue must advance plot, reveal character, express theme, and create conflict.
- No purely informational dialogue.
- Characters must not state their true thoughts directly.
- Prefer: characters discuss A while actually fighting over B.
- Advanced principle: what is said != what is wanted; what is wanted != what is done; what the audience knows != what characters say aloud.

Character Voice Engine Execution:
- You must execute characterVoiceEngine from the Story Development Layer.
- Every key dialogue beat must express the speaker's speechPattern, vocabulary, sentenceRhythm, regionalFlavor, localSpeechMarkers, and emotionalDefense.
- If character names are removed, the audience should still roughly infer who is speaking.
- Regional texture is not dialect stuffing, but local speech must not be absent. Choose expressions from localSpeechMarkers that fit the character, intensity, and scene.
- localSpeechMarkers should primarily appear through pronouns, negation, time words, question words, address terms, particles, and short connective phrases. For Wenling/Taizhou contexts, usable markers may include "nong", "ngu", "ala", "fu yao", "jin zhao", or "zhuan wu". Do not write standard Mandarin and merely claim it has regional texture.
- Keep density readable: light means 1 local marker per dialogue beat, medium means 2-3 markers, and strong may be denser. Do not translate every sentence into unreadable dialect.
- Do not let all characters share the same restrained, literary, precisely summarizing voice.
- Supporting characters especially must not speak over-generalized thematic lines.
- Do not violate any character's forbiddenStyle.

Dialogue Balance Engine:
- Dialogue must satisfy Natural Conversation + Dramatic Progression.
- No 100% plot pushing.
- No 100% casual small talk.
- Target ratio: 60-70% character goal/conflict, 30-40% natural expression.
- Every dialogue beat must include surfaceTopic, realConflict, dramaticPurpose, naturalnessScore, and conflictScore.
- surfaceTopic is what characters appear to be discussing.
- realConflict is what they are actually fighting over.
- dramaticPurpose is what the dialogue changes.

Bad direction:
Daughter: You were never there.
Father: Sorry.
Daughter: I hate you.

Good direction:
Daughter: Do you remember which grade I changed schools?
Father: Of course.
Daughter: Which year was it?
(silence)
