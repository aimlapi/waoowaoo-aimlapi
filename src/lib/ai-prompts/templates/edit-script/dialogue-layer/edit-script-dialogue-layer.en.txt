Dialogue Layer boundaries:
- This is the final structured dialogue layer before Screenplay.
- Derive key dialogue from interactionLayerPackage.interactions. Do not bypass the Dramatic Interaction Layer or invent a new conflict.
- Only handle key interactions that should be spoken. Silent, physical, or purely performative interactions may be omitted.
- dialogue is a dialogue draft, not the final full screenplay, but it must already contain readable lines, subtext, character voice, regional/dialect texture, and rhythm.
- Do not generate new scenes, new beats, new character relationships, or a new ending.
- Do not output dialectProfile, characterVoiceEngine, localSpeechMarkers, or any old Expression Layer fields.
- Do not output Story Development, Narrative Innovation, Expression Layer fields, or any old pipeline fields.

Upstream inheritance rules:
- Obey Screenplay Skeleton Layer centralDramaticQuestion, storyOriginDiagnosis, dual value oppositions, and regionalAesthetics.
- Obey 015 Value-Sequence Loop lockedValueSwingSystem, lockedRiskRewardSystem, and lockedSequenceSpine. Dialogue pressure must serve the current sequence/scene value swing and risk/reward.
- Obey 016 Character Identity & Voice Bible character identity, taste, interests, personality, defense mechanisms, behavior habits, speech traits, and pressure collapse.
- Obey Sequence Layer sequenceQuestion, turningPoint, riskRewardEscalation, and crisisSequenceDesign.
- Obey Scene Layer sceneLocationKey, sceneGoal, sceneAntagonist, outcome, turningPoint, and dramaticPhases.
- Obey Beat Layer action, reaction, actionReactionCouple, strategyChange, informationChange, subtextSeed, valuePressure, and newCondition.
- Obey Dramatic Interaction Layer spokenSurface, hiddenLayer, subtextPlan, informationControl, dialogueTactics, behavioralExpression, microTurn, and dialogueConstraint.
- If informationControl.informationChange is none, do not force a reveal. Preserve only strategy pressure, subtext, power movement, and rhythm change.

Dialogue principles:
- Every dialogueBeat must include speakerName, characterWant, tactic, subtext, dialogue, voiceExecution, surfaceTopic, realConflict, dramaticPurpose, naturalnessScore, and conflictScore.
- Dialogue must advance plot, reveal character, express theme, and create conflict.
- No purely informational dialogue.
- Characters must not state their true thoughts directly.
- Prefer: characters discuss A while actually fighting over B.
- Advanced principle: what is said != what is wanted; what is wanted != what is done; what the audience knows != what characters say aloud.
- dialogueTactics must inherit the action/reaction strategy collision. tactic, surfaceTopic, realConflict, and dramaticPurpose must trace back to interactionLayerPackage.interactions.
- hiddenLayer.subtext may only become subtext, pauses, avoidance, misdirection, ambiguity, or natural topic-shifting. It must not be said directly.
- The content in subtextPlan.mustNotSay must never appear verbatim in dialogue.

Character voice and regional/dialect rules:
- voiceExecution must explain how this beat executes character voice: sentence length, rhythm, aggression/avoidance, cultural identity, class texture, emotional defense, and speech habits.
- If Skeleton or Scene Layer provides concrete regional, acquaintance-society, industry, family-structure, dialect, or local-expression cues, dialogue must show regional/dialect texture at readable density.
- Regional texture does not mean dialect stuffing. It may appear through forms of address, particles, negation, time phrasing, kinship terms, industry terms, acquaintance-society talk, and local daily objects.
- Dialectization must serve character and conflict, and must remain readable.
- If upstream has no clear regional texture, do not invent a concrete dialect. Focus on character voice and natural spoken rhythm.
- Do not let all characters share the same restrained, literary, relationship-summarizing voice.
- Supporting characters especially must not speak over-generalized thematic lines.

Dialogue rhythm rules:
- Dialogue must satisfy Natural Conversation + Dramatic Progression.
- No 100% plot pushing.
- No 100% casual small talk.
- Target ratio: 60-70% character goal/conflict, 30-40% natural expression.
- dialogue may contain several short spoken lines, but they must belong to the same interaction strategy collision.
- dialogue may include brief exchanges among multiple characters. speakerName is the primary mover, and voiceExecution must explain how each speaker differs.
- Short films favor short lines, questions, interruptions, topic shifts, and sharp lines after silence. Avoid long explanations.

Bad direction:
Daughter: You were never there.
Father: Sorry.
Daughter: I hate you.

Good direction:
Daughter: Do you remember which grade I changed schools?
Father: Of course.
Daughter: Which year was it?
(silence)
