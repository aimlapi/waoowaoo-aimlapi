Writing rules:
- Fit the target short-film duration. Do not write a long-form story.
- Keep scene count restrained, usually 1-3 main scenes.
- Keep character count restrained, only characters needed to drive the story.
- Every scene must advance conflict, deepen a relationship, or reveal a cost.
- Use the unified scene heading format: "Scene N | INT/EXT. Location - Time". N must start at 1 and increase continuously.
- Every new scene must start with a scene heading before action or dialogue.
- Action paragraphs must contain only visible, performable plot facts that downstream assets, visual-reference cases, and storyboards can split. Do not write psychological explanation, theme summary, or director commentary.
- Dialogue paragraphs must contain only what a character actually says aloud. If tone, pause, or performance style is needed, put it in parentheses after the character name, such as "Kyle (lowering his voice):".
- You may write "Narrator (V.O.)" or "Character Name (O.S.)", but the line must be story-required spoken content. Do not write it as subtitles or screen text.
- When a character first appears, they must be identifiable from the Character List by stable identity and visual traits. Do not suddenly change a character's appearance, costume, or identity in action paragraphs.
- Actions must be concrete and visible. Avoid abstract concepts, inner-monologue exposition, or director commentary.
- Dialogue is optional; if there is no dialogue, the action must still complete the story.
- The ending must have a clear emotional landing, reveal, action result, or suspenseful pause.
- The ending must match the Story Development Package endingState. Do not create sudden fulfillment, sudden preaching, or dialogue that explains the theme.
- Do not use downstream production terms such as "shot", "close-up", "push-in", "cut", "CUT TO", "transition", "sound effect", "score", "BGM", "subtitles", or "title card".
