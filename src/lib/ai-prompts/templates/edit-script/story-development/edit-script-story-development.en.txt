Core principle:
- Never generate an outline or screenplay directly from the premise.
- Complete Story Development first, then generate Story Expansion.
- Do not skip the required order: Protagonist Analysis -> Theme Engine -> World Builder -> Location Texture Engine -> Dialect Profile Engine -> Antagonist System -> Character Network -> Character Voice Engine -> Fate Network -> Pressure Ladder -> Hard Choices -> Value Arc -> Story Expansion -> Narrative Structure.
- Each step must become the input basis for the next step. Later layers must not contradict earlier layers.
- Story Expansion may only be created after character, value conflict, antagonist system, fate network, pressure ladder, hard choices, and value arc are complete.
- Structure organizes an already developed story. It must not invent the story.
- Every turn must carry a cost. Without cost, there is no drama.
- If the story involves marriage, childbearing, lineage, poverty, age shame, or social judgment, do not reduce it to a simple scam. The relationship must test whether the protagonist seeks a person or demands a function.

Step 1 — Protagonist Analysis:
- Generate name, ageRange, socialPosition, externalState, innerWound, lie, want, and need.
- want must be an external objective that can be pursued through action and blocked by outside forces.
- need must be an internal growth need, the thing the protagonist truly lacks and initially refuses to admit.
- lie must be the protagonist's false belief, and it must cause the want.
- want and need must be in conflict.
- Forbidden: want = need.

Step 2 — Theme Engine:
- Generate valueA, valueB, centralDramaticQuestion, and controllingIdea.
- valueA and valueB must conflict, and the whole story must orbit that conflict.
- valueA and valueB must be mutually exclusive. The protagonist must not be able to fully obtain both in the key choice.
- centralDramaticQuestion must be answerable by the final choice.
- controllingIdea must answer centralDramaticQuestion.
- Do not write abstract slogans such as "love matters" or "people must be brave".
- Do not use value pairs that can be obtained together.
- Correct direction examples: career VS family, greatness VS happiness, being remembered VS being loved, freedom VS safety, dignity VS survival.

Step 3 — World Builder:
- Generate world.
- Do not use generic places such as "southern small town", "northern county town", or "some village" as the whole location design.
- world.region must specify a tangible city, county, or cultural geography. Fictional place names are allowed, but they must imply clear geography, climate, public spaces, and living habits.
- The world is not decoration. It must amplify the protagonist's want, need, and themeEngine value conflict.

Step 4 — Location Texture Engine:
- Generate locationTexture after world.
- It must include city, cityDistrict, climate, livingHabits, acquaintanceSocietyTexture, funeralSceneTexture, and visualMotifs.
- Regional texture is not the same as piling on dialect.
- Regional texture should mainly come from forms of address, daily details, scene objects, spoken rhythm, acquaintance-society interactions, and local public spaces.
- funeralSceneTexture must include local procedures, spatial objects, neighbor observation, or ritual pressure. Do not merely write "funeral home" or "old apartment".
- visualMotifs must contain at least 3 concrete motifs usable by downstream visual-reference cases, assets, and storyboards.

Step 5 — Dialect Profile Engine:
- Generate dialectProfile after locationTexture.
- dialectProfile must serve world.region, locationTexture.city, cityDistrict, and character class background. Do not borrow dialect markers from unrelated regions.
- dialectProfile must include region, dialectFamily, speechRegister, pronounMarkers, negationMarkers, timeMarkers, questionMarkers, addressTerms, particles, usageRules, and avoidMixingWith.
- pronounMarkers, negationMarkers, timeMarkers, questionMarkers, addressTerms, and particles must contain local expressions that can directly enter dialogue, not standard-language explanations.
- usageRules must define density, which characters should use light/medium/strong local speech, and how readability is preserved.
- avoidMixingWith must list dialect systems or accent types that are easy to confuse with this region but do not belong here.
- If the region cannot be pinned down to a county-level dialect, generate a credible larger dialect-family speech layer and state the uncertainty boundary.

Step 6 — Antagonist System:
- Generate embodiedAntagonist, institutionalAntagonist, and abstractAntagonist.
- embodiedAntagonist is a concrete person or concrete human pressure source.
- institutionalAntagonist is a system, such as platform algorithms, company rules, education systems, legal systems, village ethics, or industry rules.
- abstractAntagonist is an abstract force, such as time, death, being forgotten, loneliness, war, aging, or poverty.
- All three antagonist layers must actively block the protagonist's want, not merely decorate the background.

Step 7 — Character Network:
- Every major character must include dramaticFunction, themePosition, fateRepresentation, and questionToProtagonist.
- Every character must represent a value position or a possible fate.
- Do not create utility-only characters.
- Keep 2 to 6 characters, retaining only those needed to intensify value conflict and pressure.

Step 8 — Character Voice Engine:
- Generate characterVoiceEngine.
- It must cover the protagonist and every major character in characterNetwork.
- Every character must define speechPattern, vocabulary, sentenceRhythm, regionalFlavor, localSpeechMarkers, emotionalDefense, and forbiddenStyle.
- vocabulary must contain that character's forms of address, daily words, professional words, avoidance words, or connective phrases. Do not give every character the same set.
- regionalFlavor should mainly come from address terms, daily details, spoken rhythm, acquaintance-society interaction, and local public spaces. It is not dialect stuffing.
- localSpeechMarkers must be selected from dialectProfile pronounMarkers, negationMarkers, timeMarkers, questionMarkers, addressTerms, and particles. Do not invent new dialect markers outside the profile.
- localSpeechMarkers must be executable local language markers in the form "standard expression -> local/class/age speech expression from dialectProfile -> usage context -> intensity". They must cover at least 3 categories among pronouns, negation, time words, question words, address terms, and particles.
- Different regions must produce different dialectProfiles. Do not migrate examples from one region into another region. Do not merely write "slight dialect flavor".
- Keep local speech readable: protagonist and core local characters may use light/medium intensity, strongly local characters may use medium/strong intensity, but do not turn whole dialogue blocks into unreadable dialect.
- forbiddenStyle must clearly state what language does not belong to this character, such as "no literary relationship summaries", "no authorial theme statements", or "no therapeutic self-analysis".
- Do not let every character share the same restrained, literary, precisely summarizing voice.
- Supporting characters especially must not speak over-generalized thematic lines.
- If character names are removed, the audience should still roughly infer who is speaking.

Step 9 — Fate Network:
- Generate Future A, Future B, and Future C.
- Each Future must attach to one major character and answer: "If the protagonist becomes this person, what ending do they gain?"
- Different Futures must represent different life paths, not paraphrases of the same ending.

Step 10 — Pressure Ladder:
- Generate at least 4 pressure levels.
- Level N+1 must be clearly greater than Level N.
- Pressure must escalate. Do not repeat the same pressure at the same level.
- Preferred pressure order: career -> relationship -> identity -> existence.

Step 11 — Hard Choices:
- Generate at least 3 hard choices.
- Each choice must be valueA VS valueB and include decision and cost.
- Each choice must be Good vs Good or Bad vs Bad.
- Forbidden: Good vs Evil.
- Every choice must produce a real cost.

Step 12 — Value Arc:
- Generate openingBelief, closingBelief, openingValueState, and closingValueState.
- The protagonist must change.
- If opening and closing are identical, regenerate.

Step 13 — Story Expansion:
- Every movement must use goal -> pressure -> choice -> cost -> newValueState.
- Do not write a sequence of events.
- The expansion must show pressure -> choice -> cost -> change.

Step 14 — Final Story Development JSON:
- Summarize all steps.
- JSON must contain protagonist, themeEngine, world, locationTexture, dialectProfile, valueArc, antagonistSystem, characterNetwork, characterVoiceEngine, fateNetwork, pressureLadder, hardChoices, storyExpansion, and narrativeStructure.
- Any missing field is a generation failure.

Final quality requirements:
- The protagonist must be one specific person, not an event, group, or concept.
- want must be caused by lie. need must refute lie.
- themeEngine's value conflict must permeate the character network, pressure ladder, hard choices, and ending state.
- locationTexture must be specific. Do not only write generic location labels such as "southern small town" or "northern county town".
- dialectProfile must match world.region and locationTexture. Do not borrow dialect markers across regions.
- characterVoiceEngine must cover the protagonist and every major character, character voices must be distinguishable, and every character must include at least 3 executable localSpeechMarkers derived from dialectProfile.
- All three antagonist layers must exist and actively block want.
- fateNetwork must offer at least 3 different fate mirrors for the protagonist.
- hardChoices must force real costs. No easy reversals and no costless victories.
- storyExpansion must write movement-level pressure, choice, cost, and value-state change. Do not write a plot-event list.
- endingState must be restrained. Do not create sudden fulfillment or slogan-like theme closure.
